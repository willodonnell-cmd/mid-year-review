Market Context and Strategic Positioning
Entering 2026, venture capital conditions are improving as expected. 2025 marked the second-strongest year on record for U.S. venture investment with $340B deployed. The pattern has continued into 2026, with global startup funding reaching a record $510B in the first half of the year, driven largely by AI and with OpenAI and Anthropic alone accounting for 43% of all startup funding. Although overall deal activity remains below peak levels, investors are showing renewed confidence in companies that can demonstrate durable growth, capital efficiency, and a clear path to profitability. 
The clearest sign that market conditions are improving is the reopening of the liquidity window after several years of limited exits. Venture-backed IPO activity accelerated through the end of 2025 and into 2026, highlighted by public offerings from Figma, CoreWeave, Chime, and BETA Technologies, while strategic M&A returned at scale. This March, Google completed its $32B acquisition of Wiz, the largest acquisition of a venture-backed cybersecurity company, and Google's largest acquisition to date. We are seeing renewed confidence from both public investors and corporate development buyers and liquidity pathways for mature venture-backed companies. While the market remains selective, high-quality businesses are once again finding viable exit opportunities through both public and private markets. 
That improving backdrop is increasingly being reflected directly in the Prologis Ventures portfolio, with several active exit or liquidity paths underway across our most strategic companies. We expect to see several potential exits over the next six to twelve months that will drive returns for Prologis in a significant way. Read more details on anticipated outcomes below. While outcomes will remain company specific, the broader venture environment is more favorable than it has been at any point since 2021, creating increased opportunities to generate returns over the next six to twelve months.

Development 
Our portfolio company Anori, an AI platform that streamlines development and entitlement workflows, completed its nine-month spinout from Google X at the start of the year. Since then, Ventures has led working sessions with Prologis Development, Capital Deployment, Government Affairs, and Data Centers to identify the highest-value development challenges for Anori to prioritize solving. The team has helped shape the platform around entitlement analysis, zoning and permitting workflows, site feasibility, regulatory review, community sentiment, and development planning. Anori has now developed its first product, and structured validation is now underway using 2-3 Prologis projects to measure speed, accuracy, completeness, actionability, and workflow fit before determining the first repeatable production workflow. The long-term objective is to improve entitlement preparedness, reduce manual research, strengthen underwriting assumptions, and fundamentally change how development teams evaluate and prioritize projects across the portfolio. 
Additionally, Ventures continues working with Development and Prologis Labs/IT to identify where AI can create durable advantages across the development lifecycle. After evaluating more than 100 AI-native real estate companies, the team concluded that the greatest opportunity is not automating individual tasks, but building AI-enabled workflows that improve capital allocation, compress project timelines, strengthen underwriting, reduce entitlement and cost risk, and continuously improve decision-making across future developments. Current work includes a proof of concept with Ventures portfolio company Tangible as part of the Cost IQ initiative. The pilot will test whether AI can reduce by 50-90% the up to 40 hours development teams currently spend estimating construction material quantities, enabling faster project evaluation and more accurate early-stage cost forecasting.

Key Summit insights: 
	•	Automation investments are becoming more disciplined. Customers are increasingly evaluating automation based on throughput, reliability, and financial return rather than technical capabilities. Operators also emphasized the need to involve operations teams earlier in technology evaluations to improve implementation success. 
	•	The first 90 days after opening a facility represent one of the highest operational risk periods. Customers consistently cited the gap between planning assumptions and operational reality as a leading cause of launch delays. Many see simulation and pre-launch testing as opportunities to identify issues before operations begin and accelerate stabilization. 
	•	Retail compliance and chargebacks are becoming a growing source of operational cost. Customers described spending significant time and resources resolving compliance disputes with retailers, increasing interest in technologies such as computer vision and automated documentation to improve shipment accuracy and reduce chargebacks.

These customer discussions also informed broader industry research. Most recently, Ventures worked with Prologis Research and Silicon Valley Bank to produce the SVB x Prologis Ventures Physical AI and the Future of Robotics Report, extending many of the themes heard throughout the first half of the year to a broader survey of PrologisNext executives. Their anonymous contributions on AI adoption, barriers to scaling automation, and unmet technology needs, complement SVB's analysis of venture funding and broader market trends. The findings provide insight into enterprise technology investment priorities and innovation roadmaps. Key takeaways are summarized below, and the full summary of executive responses can be found here. 
	•	Warehouse operators are investing in AI where it saves time today. The most common use cases were reporting and analytics, document automation, and labor planning, while more advanced applications remain in the evaluation stage. 
	•	Customers are becoming more confident in AI and automation, but they expect measurable results. Nearly two-thirds of respondents said they are more optimistic than they were a year ago, citing improvements in safety, throughput, and labor productivity. 
	•	Customers continue to see significant room to expand automation. Even among large operators, a median of only 40% of warehouse facilities have deployed automation, indicating substantial opportunity as solutions become easier to implement across diverse operations. 
Business Building & Incubation
Building upon past incubation success, Prologis Ventures is creating an incubation engine that supports the creation of new businesses that strengthen the Prologis platform and generate new revenue. The investment in Fulfillment IQ established CrossDock Studios, our incubation studio that combines Prologis' deep understanding of customer challenges with Fulfillment IQ's product development capabilities to rapidly build and commercialize new businesses. 
Every initiative follows a structured path through evaluating customer pain points, discovery, rapid prototyping, design partner validation, and iteration. Depending on the opportunity, products may become standalone venture-backed companies, future Prologis Ventures investments, Operations Essentials offerings, or consulting solutions that support both companies. Below are the three incubations currently in progress: 
Workbench
Warehouse design remains a manual process that requires months of engineering analysis across facility layouts, labor planning, automation design, and financial modeling before a customer can make an investment decision. Workbench will dramatically accelerate that process by bringing warehouse design, simulation, and AI into a single platform. The initial release of Workbench is designed to help 3PL customers convert their customers' warehouse requirements into optimized facility designs in hours instead of months. NFI has signed as the first paying design partner with a $200k annual commitment, while GXO, DHL, Geodis, and Yusen are in discussions. Phase One is scheduled for delivery in October 2026. 
But the larger opportunity for Workbench is far more significant. Workbench will become a true digital twin and simulation engine for warehouse operations. Developed alongside NVIDIA on the Omniverse platform, Prologis will be able to simulate automation strategies, warehouse layouts, labor models, throughput, and network decisions before making physical investments. As more customer and operational data is incorporated into the platform, Workbench will become the operating system for warehouse operations. Prologis can help a customer model their entire network, compare investment scenarios, and simulate operational performance before spending a dollar on infrastructure or automation. This positions Prologis as a trusted partner in helping customers make critical decisions, creating a significant new software and advisory revenue opportunity while increasing pull-through across Operations Essentials and more.
WareSight
Every customer facility assessment and Operating Essentials product recommendation depends on the experience of the ESM conducting the walkthrough. WareSight transforms that experience into an AI-powered operational assessment. As an ESM walks a facility with a customer, the platform records the conversation, creates a voice-enabled assessment in real time, analyzes operational observations, and recommends solutions across Operations Essentials offerings. WareSight can operate even in low-connectivity environments, allowing recommendations to be generated during the customer visit. Live customer pilots are expected to begin this fall. 
Zynque
Zynque demonstrates how CrossDock Studios is able to incubate and create venture backable stand-alone businesses. Zynque is a logistics-native integration and orchestration platform designed to connect warehouse management systems, transportation systems, robotics, ERP platforms, and other warehouse technologies through a common integration layer built specifically for logistics environments. Prologis Ventures is working with FIQ to spin Zynque out of the business, taking the company to market to raise a first tranche of venture capital from both institutional and strategic investors.
